03 / 06What we used

No photos, no ground control: just one video file, turned into a map on the laptop.

  1. Pick sharp frames220 of 880 scored frames kept: the sharpest one per 2 m of flight (our standard sharpness check, not AI).
  2. Read the GPS100% of frames geotagged from the drone's own telemetry inside the video. ExifTool could not read it, so we wrote our own decoder.
  3. Build the mapGPU OpenDroneMap, quick settings, on the laptop's RTX 4050. No internet, no cloud.

05 / 06What we learnt

Open ground maps cleanly

The pitch, goals, people and tennis court came out sharp and recognisable.

Tall things smear

Buildings and trees seen at an angle streak across the map. The quick settings drape photos over a smoothed ground, so anything tall seen sideways gets stretched.

A video is not a survey

The path never revisited the same ground, so the frames formed 7 loose pieces. How you fly decides the map, more than the software does.

06 / 06How to fly it next time
  • Camera pointing straight down
  • Steady height above the ground
  • Overlapping parallel passes, like mowing a lawn
  • Daylight: at ISO 6,400 the camera's noise reduction likely softened detail
  • Lay out a few marked checkpoints so the error can be measured

That's why our accuracy numbers come from a proper survey: USGS public photos of a real debris flow.

Our own survey flights on 29–30 Oct were cancelled by rain. We'll fly one with checkpoints before the 21 Nov round.
